## Is my gig income taxable
Yes. Money earned as a delivery or ride partner is generally treated as income from business or profession, not salary, because you are an independent partner rather than an employee. You can deduct genuine costs of earning it, such as fuel, vehicle maintenance, phone and data, if you keep records.

## New tax regime rebate
Under the new tax regime, the Union Budget 2025-26 raised the rebate so that individuals with total taxable income up to ₹12 lakh in a year pay no income tax, from financial year 2025-26. Most full-time delivery and ride partners earn well below this, but filing a return is still useful: it creates an income record for loans and visas, and lets you claim refunds of any tax deducted.

## Presumptive taxation
Small businesses can opt for presumptive taxation under Section 44AD, declaring a fixed percentage of turnover as profit (6% for receipts through banks or digital modes, 8% otherwise) instead of keeping full books. Professionals use Section 44ADA. Whether these sections fit your gig income depends on how it is classified, so check with a tax professional before choosing. Presumptive returns are filed using ITR-4.

## Tax deducted at source
Some platforms or payment intermediaries deduct tax at source (TDS) from payouts. Check your Form 26AS or Annual Information Statement (AIS) on the income tax portal. You can claim that tax back as a refund when you file your return if your total tax is lower.

## Records to keep
Keep your monthly platform statements, your GigPay statement, and fuel and repair bills. The usual due date for filing an income tax return for individuals who don't need an audit is 31 July after the financial year ends (sometimes extended).
